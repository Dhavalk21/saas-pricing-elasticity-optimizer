SaaS Pricing Economics Playbook & User Manual

Strategic Framework

1 Price Elasticity of Demand (Ed)

Measures how sensitive your users are to price hikes. If Ed = 0.5 (Inelastic), a 10% price hike only cuts sales by 5%, leading to higher total revenue. If Ed = 2.0 (Elastic), a 10% price hike cuts sales by 20%, hurting revenue.

2 Discovering the "Sweet Spot"

By combining elasticity coefficients with pricing tiers, we construct an exponential decay curve. This simulates the exact pricing threshold where total recurring margins are maximized before drop-offs take over.

3 Tier Optimization

Enterprise products usually benefit from price increases because of low price elasticity, whereas consumer apps require high volume and lower prices to succeed. Use the simulator presets to see both strategies.

How to Use & Try out a Sample Example:

  1. Click the white Load Highly Inelastic (Enterprise) Scenario button at the top to load our pre-configured base enterprise settings.
  2. Note how the audience volume is small (12,000 traffic) but the base conversion is highly resilient with a low sensitivity coefficient (Ed = 0.45).
  3. In Section 2, locate the Pro Plan price slider. Move it upwards to increase the price from €79 to €95.
  4. Look at the right panel: despite a minor decay in conversion rate, the Annual Revenue Lift expands by thousands of Euros! This demonstrates an ideal high-yield monetization tweak.

Elasticity & Packaging Benchmarks:

  • Inelastic Range (Ed < 1.0): Low price sensitivity. Users value utility over cost. A great indicator of solid product-market fit or mission-critical enterprise tooling. Perfect for high-ACV expansion.
  • Elastic Range (Ed > 1.0): High price sensitivity. Common in highly saturated B2C/Consumer spaces. Price increases here should be backed by significant product value upgrades to prevent churn.
  • Ideal LTV to CAC Ratio: Target an index ≥ 3.0x. If your simulated ratios fall below 3.0x, it signifies your acquisition cost (CAC) is unsustainably high compared to long-term monetization.

SaaS Pricing Research Methodologies

Gabor-Granger Method

Pricing Elasticity Identification

Surveys customers by asking if they would purchase a product at randomly selected price points to plot an empirical demand curve. This model calculates the precise price elasticity coefficient (Ed) simulated in this dashboard.

Van Westendorp Meter

Price Sensitivity Mapping

Asks users four critical questions (Too Cheap, Cheap/Good Value, Expensive, Too Expensive) to map intersections and discover the "Range of Acceptable Prices" and the Point of Marginal Cheapness.
